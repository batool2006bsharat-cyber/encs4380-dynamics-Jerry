1. IDENTIFY THE PLANT

Measured motor model

For the Micromouse drive motor, the plant is modelled as G(s) = K / (τs + 1), where the input is PWM duty cycle (%) and the output is wheel speed (rpm). Enter the values obtained from your own encoder/PWM step test. Do not use the example values from P3 as measured results.

Published experimental reference model (not our Micromouse measurements). Von Chong et al. (2026), Data in Brief, identifies the 20 ms plant as G(s) = 25.59/(s + 22.37), with measured output in RPM and PWM command recorded in integer counts. The article's static characterization uses PWM commands from 0 to 254 counts. Therefore K_count = 25.59/22.37 = 1.14394 RPM/count and τ = 1/22.37 = 0.04470 s. Using 254 counts = 100% duty gives K = 1.14394 × 2.54 = 2.90561 RPM per 1% PWM. The dataset's motor is a TSINY TS-25GA370H-20, 12 V, nominal 300 RPM, with encoder feedback. These are converted literature-reference values from a different test platform, NOT measurements of our Micromouse. Published article · Experimental dataset.
Where do K and τ come from?

5. ENGINEERING RECORD
What to document before submission


    

  • Identify the actual motor/gearbox and encoder counts per wheel revolution, if hardware is available; otherwise clearly label the model as a published reference, not our Micromouse measurement.
    • 

  • For measured data: include PWM step magnitude and speed-versus-time log. For literature data: cite the paper and dataset and document input/output units.
    • 

  • Show how K and τ were derived or fitted; never treat normalized published model coefficients as rpm/%PWM without verifying scaling.
    • 

  • Why the selected speed setpoint and damping ratio are suitable for the Micromouse.
    • 

  • Final Kp, Ki, Kd, simulated response, and evidence that PWM remains between 0% and 100%.
